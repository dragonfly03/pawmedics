Dogs and cats are living longer than ever, and many spend a third of their lives or more as seniors. Getting older isn't an illness, but it does bring changes worth watching for. Many of them, especially pain, can be treated, so your pet stays comfortable and active for longer.

## When is a pet "older"?

It depends on the animal. Cats are usually considered senior from around **11 years old**. Dogs age at different rates depending on size: giant breeds may be senior from about 6 or 7, while small dogs often stay young until 10 or later.

Rather than focusing on the number, watch for changes in how your pet moves, eats, sleeps and behaves.

## Arthritis: the most common problem we miss

Arthritis is painful inflammation of the joints. It is very common in older dogs, and even more common in cats than many owners realise: studies show that **most cats over 12 have signs of arthritis on X-rays**. Pets rarely cry out with arthritis pain. They change how they live instead.

**Signs in dogs:**

- slow or stiff when getting up, especially after rest
- reluctant to climb stairs, jump into the car or onto the sofa
- shorter walks, lagging behind, or sitting down during walks
- limping, or stiffness that eases as they warm up
- licking a joint, or becoming grumpy when touched

**Signs in cats:**

- no longer jumping up to high places, or jumping down carefully
- using a chair as a step before climbing
- grooming less, with a scruffy or matted coat, often over the back
- having accidents outside the litter tray, which may be too high to step into
- sleeping more and playing less, or becoming withdrawn or irritable

If you notice any of these, book a check-up. Treatment may include pain relief made for pets, weight loss, controlled exercise, physiotherapy-style exercises, newer long-acting injections for arthritis pain, and [acupuncture](/advice/veterinary-acupuncture-what-to-expect/). Most pets do best with a combination.

**Never give human painkillers** such as paracetamol or ibuprofen. They can be very dangerous for dogs and cats.

## Other changes to watch for

- **Drinking and peeing more.** This can point to kidney disease, diabetes or, in cats, an overactive thyroid. Bring a urine sample if you can.
- **Weight loss or gain.** Weigh your pet regularly. Gradual weight loss in an older cat is easy to miss and often a sign of illness.
- **Bad breath, drooling or chewing on one side.** Dental disease is common and painful. Many pets eat better after dental treatment.
- **Lumps and bumps.** Most are harmless, but we should check any new lump, or one that changes.
- **Coughing, breathlessness or tiring quickly** can be signs of heart disease.
- **Confusion at night**, getting lost in corners, staring at walls or forgetting house training can be signs of age-related brain changes, similar to dementia. Some treatments help.
- **Cloudy eyes or bumping into things.** Some cloudiness is normal ageing, but sight loss should be checked.

## Check-ups for older pets

We recommend a check-up **every six months** for older dogs and cats. A year is a long time in a pet's life, and many age-related diseases can be managed well when we catch them early.

A senior check-up may include:

- a full examination, including joints, teeth, heart and weight
- a blood pressure check, especially in cats
- blood and urine tests to check the kidneys, liver and thyroid
- a review of diet, exercise and any medicines

## Making home easier

- **Rugs and runners** on smooth tiled floors give grip, which is a big help in Maltese homes with tiled floors.
- **Ramps or steps** to the sofa, bed or car.
- **A thick, supportive bed** in a warm, draught-free spot, away from stairs.
- **Low-sided litter trays** for cats, and food, water and trays on every floor of the house.
- **Raised food and water bowls** for large dogs with stiff necks or backs.
- **Shorter, more frequent walks** instead of one long one. In summer, walk early or late to avoid the heat, as older pets cope less well with high temperatures.
- **Keep them slim.** Extra weight puts more strain on painful joints.
- **Keep their minds busy** with food puzzles, sniffing games and gentle play.

## Common questions

### Is my dog just getting old, or is it in pain?

Often it's both. Stiffness, slowing down on walks and reluctance to jump are common signs of arthritis pain. A check-up can tell the difference, and treating the pain often brings back a lot of the old energy.

### How often should an older cat see the vet?

Every six months is a good routine for cats over about 11 years old. Cats hide illness well, so regular checks, weight measurements and blood tests help us find problems early.

### Can arthritis in cats be treated?

Yes. Pain relief made for cats, weight control, changes at home and other therapies such as acupuncture can help. Many cats start jumping, grooming and playing again once their pain is under control.

### What should I feed my older dog or cat?

That depends on its weight, health and any illness. Some older pets need fewer calories, while others need more protein or a special diet for their kidneys or joints. Ask us before you switch.
