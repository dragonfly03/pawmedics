Christmas is one of the busiest times of year for vets, and most of the cases are avoidable. A box of chocolates under the tree, a slice of panettone left on the coffee table, a curious cat and some tinsel. This guide covers what to watch out for, and what to do if something goes wrong.

## Festive foods that can make pets ill

**Chocolate and cocoa.** Chocolate contains theobromine, a stimulant that can cause vomiting, a racing heart, tremors and seizures. The darker the chocolate, the more theobromine it contains, so dark chocolate and cocoa powder are the most dangerous. That includes the Christmas log and a mug of imbuljuta. White chocolate contains very little theobromine but is fatty enough to upset a stomach. Dogs can sniff out wrapped chocolates under the tree, so keep gifts out of reach until they're opened.

**Grapes, raisins, sultanas and currants.** These can cause sudden kidney failure in dogs. The amount that causes harm varies between dogs and can't be predicted, so any amount should be taken seriously. Think mince pies, Christmas pudding, Christmas cake and panettone.

**Sugar-free sweets and chewing gum.** Many contain xylitol, a sweetener that is very harmful to dogs and can be fatal. It also turns up in some peanut butters and baking mixes, so check the label.

**Onions, garlic and leeks.** Common in stuffing, gravy and sauces. They can damage red blood cells in dogs and cats and lead to anaemia.

**Cooked bones and fatty leftovers.** Cooked bones splinter and can get stuck in, or tear, the gut. Turkey skin and fatty trimmings can trigger pancreatitis, a painful inflammation of the pancreas.

**Alcohol.** Including liqueur chocolates, rum-soaked cakes and unattended glasses. Pets are far more sensitive to alcohol than people.

**Macadamia nuts.** These can make dogs weak, wobbly and shaky, even in small amounts.

If you'd like to include your pet in Christmas dinner, a little plain cooked turkey breast with no skin, bones, gravy or seasoning is fine for most dogs and cats. A treat made for pets is safer still.

## Plants and decorations

**Lilies are dangerous for cats.** Every part of the plant is toxic to cats: petals, leaves, stem, pollen and even the water in the vase. A cat can be poisoned just by grooming pollen off its fur. If you have a cat, keep lilies out of the house altogether, including "pollen-free" ones.

**Tinsel, ribbon and string** are irresistible to cats and can cause a serious blockage if swallowed. If you see string hanging from your pet's mouth or bottom, don't pull it. Call us.

**Batteries.** Swallowed batteries, especially button batteries from toys and remote controls, can burn the mouth, throat and stomach.

**Baubles and fairy lights.** Glass baubles break into sharp pieces, and puppies love to chew cables. Switch lights off at the wall when you're out.

**Mistletoe and holly** can cause stomach upsets and are best kept out of reach. **Poinsettias and Christmas trees** usually cause no more than mild irritation or a tummy upset, but don't let pets chew them.

## Visitors, noise and New Year

A house full of guests, open doors and loud celebrations can be stressful for pets. Give your pet a quiet room with its bed, water and a few toys where it can retreat. Ask guests not to share food from their plates, check doors and gates as people come and go, and make sure your pet's microchip details are up to date in case it slips out.

New Year fireworks frighten many dogs and cats. If your pet struggles with loud noises, read our guide to [fireworks and frightened pets](/advice/fireworks-pets-malta/) and talk to us well before the 31st.

## If your pet eats something it shouldn't

1. **Call us straight away**, even if your pet seems fine. Some poisons, such as raisins, xylitol and rat bait, don't show signs for hours or days, and early treatment makes a real difference.
2. **Tell us what your pet ate, roughly how much, and when.** Keep the wrapper or packaging if you can, and know your pet's approximate weight.
3. **Don't try to make your pet sick at home** unless a vet tells you to. Salt water and other home remedies can cause more harm than the original problem.

## Common questions

### My dog ate one chocolate. Is that dangerous?

It depends on the type of chocolate, the amount and the size of your dog. A small dog eating dark chocolate is far more at risk than a large dog eating one milk chocolate. Call us with the wrapper and your dog's weight, and we'll tell you whether your dog needs treatment.

### Are mince pies safe for dogs?

No. Mince pies contain raisins, currants and sultanas, which can cause kidney failure in dogs. Some also contain alcohol. If your dog has eaten one, call us straight away.

### Is it safe to have lilies in the house if my cat never goes near them?

We'd advise against it. Pollen falls onto surfaces and onto your cat's fur, and cats swallow it when they groom. Even a small amount can damage the kidneys.

### Is a little turkey safe for my dog?

A small amount of plain, boneless, skinless turkey breast is fine for most dogs. Avoid the skin, fat, bones, stuffing and gravy, which can cause pancreatitis or contain onion and garlic.
