Fireworks are part of life in Malta. New Year is the big night in many countries, but here they also go off on festa weekends from late spring to the end of summer, often with loud ground petards in the village square. For a dog or cat with sensitive hearing, that can mean weeks of fear.

Preparation helps a great deal, and fear of noises can be treated.

## Signs your pet is frightened

Some pets make their fear obvious. Others show it in quieter ways. Watch for:

- trembling, panting or drooling
- pacing, barking, whining or howling
- hiding under furniture, or trying to escape
- clinging to you, or refusing to leave one spot
- refusing food, or having accidents in the house
- in cats: hiding for hours, crouching low, or running out of the house

If your pet shows any of these signs during fireworks, it's worth acting now, because fear of loud noises often gets worse each year without help.

## Weeks before: get ready

**Make a safe den.** Choose a quiet room, ideally with no outside walls or windows facing the street. Put your pet's bed in a corner, cover a crate or table with a thick blanket to make a cave, and add a favourite toy and a piece of your clothing. Let your pet use the den every day so it's a familiar, happy place before the noise starts.

**Get used to the sounds.** Sound recordings of fireworks, played very quietly at first while your pet eats or plays, can slowly teach it that the noise means nothing bad. Turn the volume up a little over days and weeks, and stop if your pet looks worried. This works best when started well before the season.

**Check the microchip.** Frightened pets bolt. Make sure your pet's microchip is registered with your current phone number, and that dogs wear a collar with an ID tag.

**Talk to us if your pet panics.** For pets with severe fear, we may suggest medication. Some medicines need to be started days before fireworks, and we may want to try a dose first, so book a consultation well ahead of New Year or the festa season. Never give your pet medicine meant for people.

## On the day

- **Walk dogs early**, in daylight, before fireworks are likely to start. Keep your dog on a lead, even if it usually comes back when called.
- **Feed a little earlier** than usual, as a nervous pet may not want to eat later.
- **Shut doors, windows and curtains**, and switch on lights so flashes are less noticeable.
- **Play background noise**, such as the television or calm music, to soften the bangs.
- **Keep cats indoors** from before dark, with a litter tray, food and water inside.
- **Bring rabbits and guinea pigs indoors** if you can, or cover hutches with a thick blanket, leaving a gap for air, and add extra bedding for them to burrow into.
- **Check the escape routes.** Make sure gates are shut and visitors know not to leave doors open.

## During the fireworks

**Let your pet choose.** If it wants to hide in its den, let it. Don't pull it out to "face" the noise.

**Comfort is fine.** You won't make a fear worse by calmly stroking or sitting with a pet that comes to you for comfort. Stay relaxed and speak quietly.

**Never punish fear.** Shouting at a dog that barks or has an accident adds to its distress.

**Offer distractions** if your pet is interested: a chew, a food puzzle or a favourite game. If it isn't interested, don't force it.

**Stay at home if you can.** On the biggest nights, many pets cope much better with someone they trust nearby.

## After the fireworks

Expect some pets to be unsettled for a day or so. Keep routines normal and walks on a lead in case there are more fireworks. If your pet is still very anxious, isn't eating, or hurt itself trying to escape, call us.

Fear of fireworks is something to plan for every year. If this season was hard, book a visit afterwards so we can make a plan for the next one, which may include a referral to a behaviour specialist.

## Common questions

### Will comforting my dog make it more scared of fireworks?

No. Fear is an emotion, not a trick you can reward. Calmly comforting a dog that comes to you can help it feel safer. What helps most is staying calm yourself.

### When should I talk to the vet about fireworks medication?

At least two to three weeks before the event, and earlier for the festa season. That gives time to check your pet's health, choose the right medicine and try it before the night.

### Do calming collars and sprays work?

Some pets seem to benefit from pheromone diffusers, collars or sprays, but they rarely work on their own for a very frightened pet. Use them alongside a safe den and good preparation, and talk to us if your pet still struggles.

### My cat runs outside when fireworks start. What should I do?

Keep your cat indoors from before dark on fireworks nights, with a litter tray, food and water. Block cat flaps, and give your cat high and hidden places to retreat to, such as the top of a wardrobe or a box under the bed.
