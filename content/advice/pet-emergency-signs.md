It's not always easy to know whether your pet needs a vet tonight or can wait until the morning. Pets, and cats in particular, are good at hiding illness and pain. This guide lists the signs that need urgent help, so you can act quickly and calmly.

**If you're not sure, call us.** A quick phone call can save precious time, and we'll never mind being asked.

## Call a vet immediately if your pet has:

**Breathing problems.** Struggling to breathe, breathing noisily, gasping, or breathing with its mouth open (in cats). Blue, grey or very pale gums.

**Collapsed or can't stand.** Sudden weakness, fainting, or not being able to get up.

**A seizure.** A fit lasting more than five minutes, or more than one fit in a day. Keep your hands away from your pet's mouth, move furniture out of the way, and call us.

**Heavy bleeding** that doesn't slow down after five minutes of firm pressure with a clean cloth.

**A swollen, tight belly with retching.** A dog trying to vomit but bringing up nothing, or only froth, with a swollen belly, may have bloat (a twisted stomach). This needs surgery quickly and is most common in large, deep-chested breeds.

**Straining to pee with little or nothing coming out.** Especially in male cats, this can mean the bladder is blocked. A blocked cat can become critically ill within a day or two. Signs can look like constipation: repeated trips to the litter tray, crying, or licking the genitals.

**Eaten something poisonous.** Rat bait, slug pellets, human medicines, chocolate, grapes or raisins, xylitol, lilies (in cats) or antifreeze. Call before signs appear. See our guide to [common poisons](/advice/pet-poisoning-malta/).

**Overheated.** Heavy panting, drooling, wobbliness or collapse on a hot day. Start cooling your pet straight away with water before you travel. Our [summer heat guide](/advice/heatstroke-pets-summer-malta/) explains how.

**Had an accident.** Hit by a car, fallen from a height, or attacked by another dog, even if your pet seems fine. Internal injuries aren't always visible.

**An eye injury.** A swollen, bulging or cloudy eye, or a pet that suddenly keeps an eye shut.

**Trouble giving birth.** Strong straining for 30 minutes without a puppy or kitten being born, or more than two hours between births.

**Severe pain.** Crying out, not letting you touch it, hunched posture, or restlessness it can't settle from.

**A sudden allergic reaction.** A swollen face, lumps all over the body, or vomiting after a sting, especially with breathing changes.

## Call us the same day if your pet has:

- vomiting or diarrhoea that lasts more than a day, or contains blood
- not eaten for more than a day (or a rabbit that hasn't eaten for a few hours)
- become very quiet, hides, or isn't acting like itself
- started drinking or peeing much more than usual
- a new lameness it won't put weight on
- a wound, bite or abscess
- had diarrhoea or vomiting and is a very young, very old or already unwell pet

If you're not sure which group your pet falls into, call us.

## What to do in an emergency

1. **Stay calm and keep your pet still.** Move it as little as possible, especially after an accident. Slide a small pet onto a towel or into a carrier, and support a larger dog's body with a blanket used as a stretcher.
2. **Call ahead.** Ring us during opening hours so we can get ready. Out of hours, call the emergency helpline (below).
3. **Be careful.** Even gentle pets may bite when they're in pain or frightened. Handle them gently and keep your face away from theirs.
4. **Bring what helps.** Any packaging of something your pet ate, its medicines, and a note of when the problem started.
5. **Don't give food, water or medicine** unless a vet tells you to.

## Common questions

### How do I know if my pet needs an emergency vet?

If your pet has trouble breathing, has collapsed, is bleeding heavily, has a seizure, has a swollen belly while trying to vomit, can't pee, or has eaten something poisonous, it needs a vet now. If you're unsure, call. A vet can tell you over the phone whether to come in.

### What should I do if my pet needs a vet at night in Malta?

Call the national veterinary emergency helpline on 153 and choose extension 22. It's open 24 hours a day and will direct you to the emergency clinic on duty.

### Is vomiting an emergency in dogs?

A single episode in a dog that is otherwise bright usually isn't. It is urgent if your dog vomits repeatedly, tries to vomit but nothing comes up, has a swollen belly, has blood in the vomit, may have eaten something toxic or a toy, or seems unwell.

### Why is a male cat straining to pee an emergency?

Male cats have a narrow urethra that can become blocked by crystals or debris. When a cat can't pass urine, toxins build up in the blood quickly, which can be fatal within a day or two without treatment.
