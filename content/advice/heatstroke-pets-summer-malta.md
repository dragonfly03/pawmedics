Maltese summers are long and hot, and many days stay warm and humid well into the night. Dogs cool down mainly by panting, which works poorly in heat and humidity. Heatstroke can develop in minutes and can be fatal, but it is almost always preventable.

## Which pets are most at risk?

- **Flat-faced breeds** such as French bulldogs, pugs, bulldogs, boxers, Shih Tzus and Persian cats, which struggle to pant efficiently
- **Overweight** pets
- **Older pets**, and those with heart or breathing problems
- **Puppies**
- **Thick-coated** breeds such as huskies and chow chows
- **Very active dogs** that keep running or playing even when they're too hot
- **Rabbits and guinea pigs**, which can't sweat or pant well and overheat quickly in hutches in the sun

## Preventing heatstroke

**Walk at the coolest times.** In summer, walk early in the morning or late in the evening. Skip the walk on very hot days and play gentle games indoors instead. Your dog won't mind missing a walk, but it can suffer from overheating.

**Test the pavement.** Put the back of your hand on the ground for seven seconds. If it's too hot for your hand, it's too hot for paws. Tarmac, stone and sand get very hot in the Maltese sun and can burn pads.

**Never leave a pet in a car**, even for a few minutes, in the shade or with the windows open. The inside of a car heats up far faster than the air outside.

**Provide shade and water.** Make sure your pet always has a cool, shaded place and plenty of fresh water, both indoors and outside. Bring water on walks and to the beach.

**Keep homes cool.** Draw curtains and shutters in the day, use fans or air conditioning, and let your pet lie on cool tiles. Cooling mats and a damp towel to lie on can help.

**Help rabbits and guinea pigs.** Move hutches into full shade, ideally indoors in the hottest weeks. Put a frozen water bottle wrapped in a towel inside for them to lie against.

**Trim, don't shave.** A trim can help long-haired dogs, but don't shave double-coated breeds to the skin, as their coat protects against sunburn and heat.

**Protect from sunburn.** Pets with white or thin fur, especially on the ears and nose, can burn. Ask us about pet-safe sun cream.

## Signs of heatstroke

Early signs:

- heavy, fast panting that doesn't settle with rest
- drooling, thick saliva
- red gums and tongue
- restlessness, or looking for cool places
- tiring quickly or lying down on a walk

Serious signs:

- wobbliness or confusion
- vomiting or diarrhoea
- dark red, purple or very pale gums
- collapse or seizures

## What to do: cool first, transport second

If your dog shows signs of overheating, **start cooling it straight away**, before you set off for the vet. Research from the Royal Veterinary College shows that fast cooling saves lives, and that cold water is safe.

1. **Move your dog out of the heat** into shade or an air-conditioned room.
2. **Cool with water.** For a young, healthy dog that is awake and alert, the fastest method is to put it in cool or cold water, such as a bath, paddling pool or the sea at a safe spot, keeping its head above water. For older, unwell or collapsed dogs, pour or spray water all over the body instead, and keep the air moving with a fan or air conditioning.
3. **Use whatever water you have.** Cold is best, but any water cooler than your dog helps.
4. **Don't cover your dog with a wet towel** and leave it there. Towels trap heat once they warm up.
5. **Offer small sips of water** if your dog is alert, but don't force it to drink.
6. **Call us** while you cool, and come in once your dog's breathing starts to settle. If you have to travel, keep the car's air conditioning on full or the windows open.

Even if your dog seems to recover, it needs a check. Heatstroke can damage the organs and cause problems hours later.

## Cats in the heat

Cats are better at coping with heat than dogs, but they can still overheat, especially flat-faced, older or overweight cats. A cat panting with its mouth open is a warning sign. Move it somewhere cool, wet its fur with cool water, and call us.

## Common questions

### How hot is too hot to walk a dog in Malta?

There's no single number, as humidity, sun and your dog's breed all matter. As a guide, on summer days walk only early in the morning or late in the evening. If the pavement is too hot for the back of your hand after seven seconds, it's too hot for your dog.

### Is it safe to use cold water on an overheated dog?

Yes. Research shows that cold water is safe and is the fastest way to cool a dog with heatstroke. The old advice to use only lukewarm water is out of date. Use whatever water is available, as long as it's cooler than your dog.

### Can I take my dog to the beach in summer?

Yes, early in the morning or in the evening, with shade, plenty of fresh water and rinses with fresh water after a swim. Stop your dog from drinking seawater, which can make it ill. Check local rules, as dogs aren't allowed on some beaches in the bathing season.

### My dog overheated but seems fine now. Does it still need a vet?

Yes. Heatstroke can damage the kidneys, liver, gut and blood clotting, and some problems only show hours later. Call us for advice even if your dog looks back to normal.
