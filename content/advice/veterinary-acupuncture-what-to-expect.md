Many owners have never seen acupuncture used on an animal, so it's natural to wonder what happens and whether your pet will cope. Here is what a typical course of treatment at PawMedics looks like, from the first consultation to follow-up sessions.

Acupuncture at PawMedics is given by [Dr. Teresa Gatt](/acupuncture/), a veterinary surgeon trained in Traditional Chinese Veterinary Medicine (TCVM).

## What is veterinary acupuncture?

Acupuncture involves placing very fine, sterile needles at specific points on the body. In veterinary medicine it's mostly used to help with pain, stiffness and recovery. Research suggests that needling these points affects the nerves and the way the body processes pain signals.

It is used **alongside** modern veterinary medicine, not instead of it. Your pet can usually keep taking its normal medication, and acupuncture may sometimes help a pet manage on a lower dose, though any change to medication should always be agreed with your vet first.

Veterinary acupuncture should only be given by a qualified vet, because a diagnosis has to come first. Pain or lameness can have many causes, and some of them need other treatment.

## Which pets can it help?

Acupuncture is most often used for:

- arthritis and stiff joints in older dogs and cats
- back, neck and spinal problems
- long-term pain that is hard to control with medicine alone
- rehabilitation after surgery or injury

We treat dogs, cats and rabbits. The scientific evidence is still growing, and it is strongest for pain. We'll tell you whether we think acupuncture is likely to help your pet, and what else should be part of the plan.

## Step 1: The first consultation

Your first visit is a full veterinary consultation. Dr. Teresa will:

- go through your pet's history, medication and any X-rays or test results
- examine your pet, including how it walks, sits and moves
- check that acupuncture is suitable and safe for your pet
- explain the plan and how many sessions she expects your pet to need

Please bring any records from other vets, and a list of the medicines and supplements your pet takes. If you can, take a short video at home of what worries you, such as your dog struggling to get up, as pets often behave differently at the clinic.

## Step 2: The treatment session

Sessions take place in a quiet room, and you can stay with your pet. Your pet lies or sits on a soft mat in whatever position it finds comfortable.

The needles are much finer than the needles used for injections. Most pets barely notice them going in. They are usually left in place for around 10 to 20 minutes, while your pet rests. Many pets become calm within a few minutes, and some fall asleep.

Depending on your pet's needs, the plan may include:

- **dry needling**, using needles alone
- **electro-acupuncture**, where a very mild electrical current is passed between some of the needles
- advice on exercise, weight and changes at home that support the treatment

If your pet becomes uncomfortable or restless at any point, we stop or adjust. We never force a pet to stay still.

## Step 3: Follow-up and review

Acupuncture usually works over a course of sessions rather than in one visit. A common pattern is one session a week for the first few weeks, then spacing sessions further apart once your pet improves. Some pets with long-term conditions such as arthritis come in for top-up sessions every few weeks or months.

We'll track how your pet is moving and feeling at every visit, and change the plan if needed. If there's no improvement after the first few sessions, we'll talk about other options.

## After a session

Most pets go home relaxed. Some are a little sleepy or tired for the rest of the day, which is normal. Occasionally a pet seems slightly stiffer the day after an early session before it improves. Let us know if anything worries you.

Keep to your pet's usual routine, and offer water and a comfortable place to rest.

## Is acupuncture safe?

In trained hands, acupuncture has very few side effects. We take extra care, or avoid certain points, in pets that are pregnant, have bleeding problems, skin infections at the needle sites, or some types of tumour. That's one reason a full examination always comes first.

## Common questions

### Does acupuncture hurt my pet?

Most pets barely react. The needles are very fine and smooth, unlike injection needles. A few pets notice a needle in a sensitive area, and we adjust or move on.

### How many acupuncture sessions will my pet need?

It depends on the condition. Many pets start with weekly sessions for a few weeks, then less often as they improve. Dr. Teresa will give you an expected plan at the first consultation.

### Can my pet have acupuncture while on pain medication?

Yes. Acupuncture is usually given alongside your pet's normal medication. Never stop or lower a medicine without talking to your vet first.

### Can cats and rabbits have acupuncture?

Yes. Many cats and rabbits relax well during sessions. We adapt the number of needles and the length of the session to suit smaller and more nervous patients.
