Congratulations on your new puppy or kitten. The first year sets up your pet's health for life, and it's also the year with the most appointments. This guide explains what each one is for, so you know what to expect.

Every pet is different, so we'll tailor the plan at your first visit. You can [book a puppy or kitten check]({{book}}) online.

## The first visit

Bring your new pet in within the first few days, even if it seems healthy. At the first visit we will:

- give a full health check, from the heart and lungs to the eyes, ears, teeth and skin
- start or continue the vaccination course
- plan worming, flea, tick and sandfly protection
- check for a microchip, or fit one
- talk about feeding, toilet training, socialising and insurance
- answer all your questions

Bring any paperwork from the breeder, shelter or previous vet, especially vaccination and worming records.

## Vaccinations

Young animals get some protection from their mother's milk, but it fades over the first few months and can stop early vaccines from working. That's why puppies and kittens need a **course** of vaccines rather than a single injection.

International guidelines from the World Small Animal Veterinary Association recommend a dose every two to four weeks, with **the final dose at 16 weeks of age or older**, then a **booster at about six months** (26 weeks) instead of waiting until the first birthday. After that, adult pets need regular boosters, and we'll tell you which vaccines are due each year.

**Puppies** are vaccinated against:

- canine distemper
- canine adenovirus (infectious hepatitis)
- canine parvovirus

We'll also advise on leptospirosis and kennel cough, depending on your puppy's lifestyle, and on the vaccine against leishmaniasis once your puppy is old enough. Rabies vaccination is needed before a dog travels abroad.

**Kittens** are vaccinated against:

- feline panleukopenia (feline parvovirus)
- feline herpesvirus and feline calicivirus (cat flu)

We usually also recommend vaccinating kittens against **feline leukaemia virus (FeLV)**, especially if your kitten will go outside or live with cats that do.

**Until the course is complete**, carry your puppy rather than putting it down in public places, and keep it away from unvaccinated dogs. Gentle socialising at home, with healthy vaccinated dogs and with lots of people, is still very important at this age.

## Worming

Most puppies and kittens are born with roundworms or pick them up from their mother's milk. Some of these worms can also infect people, especially children.

European guidelines recommend:

- **Puppies:** from 2 weeks of age, every 2 weeks until 2 weeks after weaning, then monthly until 6 months old.
- **Kittens:** from 3 weeks of age, every 2 weeks until weaning, then monthly until 6 months old.

After six months we'll set a routine based on your pet's lifestyle, for example whether it hunts, eats raw food or lives with small children.

## Fleas, ticks and sandflies

Malta's warm climate means fleas and ticks are active for much of the year, and sandflies spread **leishmaniasis**, a serious disease in dogs. We'll recommend products that suit your pet's age and weight.

Never use a dog product on a cat. Many contain ingredients such as permethrin that can kill cats. Read more in our guide to [ticks, fleas and sandflies in Malta](/advice/ticks-fleas-sandflies-dogs-malta/).

## Microchipping

In Malta, the law requires every **dog to be microchipped by the time it is 4 months old**, and the chip is registered on the national database. Microchipping cats isn't compulsory, but we strongly recommend it. A microchip is the most reliable way to get a lost pet home.

The chip is about the size of a grain of rice and is placed under the skin between the shoulders, in a few seconds, without anaesthetic. Keep your contact details up to date whenever you move or change your phone number.

## Neutering

Neutering prevents unwanted litters and can lower the risk of some diseases and behaviour problems. The best age depends on the species, breed, size and sex of your pet. Cats are often neutered at around four to six months. For dogs, especially larger breeds, we may suggest waiting longer. We'll talk it through with you and help you decide.

## Common questions

### When can my puppy go out for walks?

Usually one to two weeks after the final puppy vaccine, which is given at 16 weeks or older. Until then, carry your puppy in public, and let it meet healthy, vaccinated dogs in safe places such as a friend's garden. We'll confirm the date at your puppy's last vaccination.

### Does my kitten need vaccinations if it stays indoors?

Yes. Indoor cats can still catch viruses such as panleukopenia and cat flu, which can be carried in on shoes and clothes. Your kitten also needs protection in case it gets out, goes to a cattery or meets a new cat.

### Is microchipping compulsory in Malta?

For dogs, yes. Maltese law requires dogs to be microchipped by 4 months of age. It isn't compulsory for cats, but we recommend it for every cat.
