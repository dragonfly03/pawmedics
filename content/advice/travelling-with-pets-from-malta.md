More families in Malta travel or relocate with their pets than ever before. The rules aren't difficult, but the order and timing matter, and a missed step can mean your pet is refused travel or held in quarantine. This guide covers the basics for dogs and cats. Rules change, so we'll always check the latest requirements for your destination when you book.

## The basics for travel within the EU

To travel with your dog or cat between Malta and another EU country, your pet needs:

1. **A microchip.** It must be fitted before, or on the same day as, the rabies vaccine. A rabies vaccine given before the microchip doesn't count.
2. **A rabies vaccination**, given from 12 weeks of age.
3. **A 21-day wait.** After your pet's first rabies vaccination, you must wait 21 days before travelling. Booster vaccines given on time don't need a new wait.
4. **An EU pet passport**, issued by your vet, which records the microchip, the rabies vaccination and other treatments. Under EU rules that came into force in April 2026, an EU pet passport is required for pets of EU residents travelling between EU countries.

You can travel with up to five pets under these rules. More than five counts as a commercial movement, which has stricter rules, unless you're travelling to a competition or show.

## Coming back to Malta (or arriving for the first time)

**Tapeworm treatment for dogs.** Malta is one of the few countries, with Finland, Ireland, Northern Ireland and Norway, that requires dogs to be treated for the tapeworm *Echinococcus multilocularis* before arrival. A vet must give the treatment **between 24 and 120 hours (1 to 5 days) before your dog arrives in Malta**, and record it in the passport. This doesn't apply to cats. Plan the vet visit around your flight or ferry time, as a treatment given too early or too late doesn't count.

**Tell the authorities you're coming.** Pet owners must notify the Veterinary Regulation Directorate before a pet arrives in Malta, using the online [pets arrival notification](https://www.servizz.gov.mt/en/services/web-02315). Print the confirmation and carry it with you.

**Register your dog.** If you're moving to Malta with a dog, it will need to be registered locally. Ask us when you arrive.

## Travel to the UK

Great Britain has its own rules. Your pet still needs a microchip and rabies vaccination, and an EU pet passport or an animal health certificate. Dogs coming to Great Britain directly from Malta don't need tapeworm treatment, but dogs coming from most other countries do. Check the [UK government's pet travel pages](https://www.gov.uk/bring-pet-to-uk/tapeworm-treatment-dogs) before you book.

## Travel outside the EU

Many countries outside the EU have different rules. Some need import permits, extra vaccines, health certificates signed by a government vet, or blood tests.

To come back into the EU from some countries, your pet needs a **rabies antibody blood test**, taken at least 30 days after the rabies vaccine, followed by a **three-month wait** before it can enter. If you're planning a long trip or a move outside the EU, talk to us **at least four to six months before you go**.

## Health risks abroad

Some diseases and parasites that are rare in Malta are common elsewhere. Ask us about protection before you go, especially against:

- **ticks and the diseases they carry**, common across southern Europe
- **heartworm**, spread by mosquitoes in parts of Italy and other countries
- **lungworm and other worms** in some parts of Europe

### Processionary caterpillars in mainland Europe

If you're taking your dog to Spain, France, Italy or other parts of mainland Europe in late winter or spring, watch out for **pine processionary caterpillars**. They nest in pine trees and march along the ground in long lines, usually from about February to May. Their tiny hairs cause severe, painful swelling of the mouth and tongue if a dog sniffs, licks or eats one. Keep your dog on a lead near pine trees in that season.

If your dog touches one, rinse its mouth gently with plenty of water without rubbing, wear gloves, and go to the nearest vet straight away.

## Getting your pet ready for the trip

- **Check airline rules early.** Airlines have their own rules for pets in the cabin or hold, and some don't carry flat-faced breeds in the hold.
- **Get your pet used to its carrier** weeks before, with treats and short practice trips.
- **Book a health check** before you go, especially for older pets or those with health problems.
- **Pack your pet's paperwork**, medicines, food, a lead, poo bags, and a recent photo in case it gets lost.
- **Don't sedate your pet** for travel unless a vet has examined it and recommended it. Sedation can be dangerous during flights.

## Travel planning timeline

| When | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 4 to 6 months before (outside the EU) | Travel consultation, rabies vaccine, blood test if needed |
| At least 1 month before (within the EU) | Microchip check, rabies vaccine, EU pet passport |
| 21 days before (at the latest) | First rabies vaccine must be given by now |
| A few weeks before | Check airline rules, parasite protection, carrier training |
| 1 to 5 days before arriving in Malta | Tapeworm treatment for dogs, recorded by a vet |
| Before you travel to Malta | Submit the pets arrival notification online |

## Common questions

### How long does it take to get a pet passport in Malta?

The passport itself can be issued at the same visit as the microchip check and rabies vaccine. But after the first rabies vaccine your pet must wait 21 days before it can travel, so allow at least a month.

### Does my cat need tapeworm treatment to enter Malta?

No. The tapeworm treatment rule for Malta applies to dogs only. Cats still need a microchip, rabies vaccination and a pet passport.

### Can I travel with my puppy or kitten?

Not until it's old enough. The rabies vaccine can be given from 12 weeks of age, and your pet must then wait 21 days, so most puppies and kittens can't travel within the EU until they're about 15 weeks old.

### Is my pet's old EU pet passport still valid?

Usually yes, if you live in the EU and the vaccinations are up to date. Bring it to your appointment and we'll check that everything is recorded correctly.
